# Starter Tall Tales

Everybody bluffs at once. Each round asks one question whose true answer sounds made up: you write a convincing lie against the clock, then everybody votes for the truth among a handful of those lies, and for the funniest one. You score for finding the truth and for every player your tall tale fools. There are no pieces and no turns, and whoever is at the table when the host starts the match plays.

The host chooses one shared content language from the package's real decks. Everybody bluffs about the same question, even when their interface languages differ.

The host opens each round, and the question is read when the writing clock starts. Your tall tale is saved as you type; finishing early moves the round on as soon as everybody has. A ballot asks two questions, one group of radio buttons each: which line is the truth, and which of the rest is the funniest. At a big table the first vote only narrows the field and a final decides it. The host moves the table past each reveal.

## Playing with a screen reader

Focus enters your own job: your text box while writing, your ballot while voting, the results at the reveal, and Escape always returns to it. In the text box, Enter focuses the finish button without pressing it; the surface's letter shortcuts never fire from inside it. On a ballot, the arrow keys move between the lines of a group and the second group appears once the first is marked. Press P to hear the question, R for the remaining time, S for your score and place, Shift+S for the leaders, T for what round this is and what you should be doing, Ctrl+F6 to cycle panels and Ctrl+Shift+R for text chat.

## Help layers

- **F1** opens this guide.
- **Ctrl+F1** shows the active shortcuts.
- **Ctrl+Shift+F1** reads the effective rules.
- **Ctrl+F6** cycles the game, players and connection panels.
- **Ctrl+Shift+R** opens the text chat.
