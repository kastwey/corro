# Letter Rush

Everyone writes at the same time. A round deals one letter and twelve categories, and every answer you write must start with that letter. You get three minutes. The round's judge does not write: once the clock runs out, they rule on what everybody else wrote.

## The round's judge

One player judges each round, and the job rotates so everybody judges the same number of times. The judge:

- starts the clock when the table has read the letter and the categories;
- waits while the others write;
- rules on every answer once the writing stops.

The judge writes nothing that round, which is exactly why they can rule on the answers without ruling on their own.

## Writing

The host chooses one shared content language when creating the game and may change it in the waiting room. Everyone writes to the same categories, in the same language, while buttons and help remain in each player's own interface language.

Your sheet is one text box per category, each labelled with its category. Type one answer in each. Enter moves to the next box, and from the last box it takes you to **I have finished**.

- Your answers are saved as you type them; you never have to send them.
- **I have finished** ends your writing early. When everybody has finished, the round moves on without waiting for the clock.
- Press **L** for the round's letter and **R** for the time left. Nothing counts down out loud.
- Inside an answer box those letters type themselves, so there they are **Ctrl+Shift+L** and **Ctrl+Shift+X**. The shortcut list (**Ctrl+F1**) shows both forms side by side.

## Judging

The judge works through the categories one at a time. For each one they see every answer, and each answer can be marked:

- **Valid** — it counts, and scores a point.
- **Repeated** — somebody else wrote the same thing, so nobody scores it.
- **Invalid** — it does not answer the category, or it does not start with the letter.

Corro pre-marks each answer with what it can work out on its own: a blank answer, an answer that does not start with the round's letter, and answers that match each other. Those are suggestions, not rulings — the judge can change any of them, and only the judge's decision counts. When the category is right, **Confirm this category** applies it and moves to the next.

Everyone at the table sees the same answers during this phase and hears the result of each category. Each writer also hears privately what happened to their own answer.

## Playing with a screen reader

Focus enters whatever this round has given you: your sheet while the clock runs, the answers while they are being judged. **Escape** from anywhere on the surface always comes back to it.

- While writing, **Enter** moves to the next category and, from the last one, to **I have finished** — it focuses that button rather than pressing it, so ending your round early is never an accident.
- Your answers are saved as you type. There is nothing to submit, and nothing is lost if the clock beats you to the last word.
- The letters this surface uses as commands are typed normally inside your answer boxes; they only act as commands when you are not in a text field.
- While judging, **Up** and **Down** move between the answers, and each one reads as a single sentence: who wrote it, what they wrote, whether it starts with the round's letter and whether anybody else wrote the same. Those last two are observations, not verdicts.
- **Right** enters the three verdicts for the answer you are on — valid, invalid, repeated — and **Left** or **Escape** comes back out. The verdict in force says so when you reach it, so the marking never depends on seeing a colour.
- Each category opens already marked with what Corro could work out on its own. Change anything you disagree with; only your ruling counts.
- Press **S** on the game surface to hear your score and your role, and **Shift+S** to hear the other players' scores.
- Press **L** for the round's letter and **R** for the seconds remaining; inside an answer box use **Ctrl+Shift+L** and **Ctrl+Shift+X**, which do the same from exactly there. What you asked for is also shown, quietly, so a player who is watching rather than listening gets the answer too.
- Press **T** to hear which round this is, its letter and what you should be doing.
- **Tab** and **Shift+Tab** still move through the surface's controls. Controls that cannot act remain focusable and explain what is missing.
- An audible clock ticks while the round is being written and follows the normal sound mute and volume setting. It never announces every second, and the visual progress bar is hidden from assistive technology so it cannot beep its way through the round; use **R** whenever you need the exact time.
- **Ctrl+F6** and **Ctrl+Shift+F6** move through the game, players and connection panels.
- **Ctrl+Shift+R** opens text chat. Voice chat has its own controls and remains optional.

Each category's result is announced to the whole table, and you also hear privately what happened to your own answer — which is the part no summary can carry.

## Help layers

## Winning

Each valid answer scores one point. When everybody has judged once, the highest score wins. If the lead is tied, the match adds another complete rotation and plays on until somebody wins outright.

## Leaving

If the judge leaves mid-round, the round starts again with the next player judging: the answers were written for a table that no longer exists. If so many players leave that fewer than three remain, the match ends and the leader takes it.
