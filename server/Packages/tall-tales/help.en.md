# Tall Tales

Everybody bluffs at once. Each round asks one question whose true answer sounds made up. You write a convincing lie against the clock; then everybody votes for the truth among a handful of those lies, and for the funniest. You score for finding the truth and for every player your tall tale fools. There are no pieces, no turns and no limit on how many people can sit at the table: whoever is in when the host starts the match plays.

## A round

1. The host opens the round. The question is read the moment the writing clock starts, so everyone gets the same seconds to think.
2. **Writing.** Type one tall tale in your text box. It is saved as you type; **I have finished** closes it early. If you happen to write the truth, Corro tells you privately and asks for another lie. When everyone has finished, the vote opens without waiting out the clock.
3. **Voting.** Your ballot carries the truth and up to seven tall tales, never your own. First mark the one you take for the truth; then, of the ones you did not believe, mark the funniest. Both marks save at once, and you may change either while the vote is open.
4. **The final.** At a big table, where every tall tale reached fewer than half the room, the six tales the room liked most come back with the truth for one more vote, and everybody votes again. If you already found the truth, the final asks you instead which of the finalists would have fooled you.
5. **The reveal.** The room hears the truth and how many found it, the tale that fooled the most people and whose it was, and the funniest and whose it was. You also hear, privately, what your own round came to. The host then moves on to the next round, or ends the match after the last one.

## Scoring

- Finding the truth scores 1000 points. With a final, finding it in the final scores 500.
- Every player your tall tale fools scores you 500 points. With a final, a vote in the first round pays 250 and a vote in the final pays 500.
- When several people wrote the same tall tale, it is shown once, with all of their names, and its points are divided between them. An original tale that fools ten is worth more than an obvious one that twenty wrote.
- The funniest tale earns the honour and the laugh, not points.
- After the agreed rounds, the highest score wins. A tie at the top adds one more round.

## Fairness

Every tall tale is dealt onto practically the same number of ballots, so no tale goes unread and every tale has the same chance to fool. Nothing depends on how fast you read or answer: the clocks are the same for everyone, and nobody scores for being first.

## Playing with a screen reader

Focus lands on whatever your job is: your text box while writing, your ballot while voting, the results at the reveal. **Escape** from anywhere on the surface always takes you back there.

- While writing, your tall tale is saved as you type; there is nothing to send. **Enter** in the text box takes you to **I have finished** and focuses it rather than pressing it, so ending your round early is never an accident.
- Your ballot is two groups of radio buttons, one question each. **Arrow keys** move between the lines of a group; the second group appears once you have marked the first. Each line reads as one sentence and nothing else: the tales carry no names until the reveal.
- Press **S** on the game surface to hear your score and your job, and **Shift+S** to hear the leaders.
- Press **P** to hear the round's question again, and **R** for the seconds left on the clock. Inside your text box those letters type themselves, so press **Ctrl+Shift+Space** first and then the letter: the next keystroke is a command instead of a character. What you asked for is also shown on screen, quietly, for whoever is looking rather than listening.
- Press **T** to hear which round this is and what you are supposed to be doing.
- **Tab** and **Shift+Tab** still walk the surface's controls. A control that cannot act stays focusable and says why.
- An audible clock ticks while a timed phase runs and follows the usual mute and volume settings. It never announces every second, and the visual progress bar is hidden from assistive technology so it does not beep through the whole round; press **R** when you want the exact time.
- **Ctrl+F6** and **Ctrl+Shift+F6** cycle the game, players and connection panels.
- **Ctrl+Shift+R** opens the text chat. Voice chat has its own controls and stays optional.

The reveal is spoken to the whole room, and your own result reaches you privately, which is the part no summary can carry.

## Help layers

- **F1** opens this guide.
- **Ctrl+F1** shows the active shortcuts.
- **Ctrl+Shift+F1** reads the effective rules: the clocks, the rounds and the points.

## If somebody leaves

A player who leaves takes their tall tale and their ballot with them, so no phase ever waits on somebody who is not there. If the host leaves, the next player in seat order runs the table. If so many leave that fewer than three remain, the match ends and the leader takes it.
