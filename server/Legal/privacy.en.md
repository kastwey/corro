# Privacy

This deployment of Corro is run by **{{controller}}**, established in {{jurisdiction}}, who decides
what happens to the data described below and can be reached at **{{contact}}**.

Under data-protection law that makes them the *controller*. Corro is the software; this notice is
about the person running this particular copy of it.

## Playing without an account

You do not need an account to play, and if you never sign in, nothing here identifies you.

Taking a seat at a table stores, with that table: the **name you typed**, a secret that proves the
seat is yours, and a re-entry code. If you use the chat, your messages are kept with the table too,
so that reconnecting does not wipe the conversation for everybody. The name is whatever you chose —
it does not have to be your real one.

Your browser also keeps a short list of the tables you are sitting at, plus preferences like the
theme, the volume of each voice, and which microphone you picked. That list never leaves your
browser unless you sign in, and clearing your browser's storage for this site removes it.

## If you sign in

Signing in creates an account, and the account holds:

- The **display name** shown to other players, taken from your provider's profile and editable
  afterwards.
- Your **email address**, for display only, so you can tell which login you used.
- For each sign-in method you use, the pair the provider gives us: **which provider it was, and the
  opaque identifier it assigns you**. Never a password — this deployment never sees one.
- The dates the account was created and last used.

Your account is *not* matched by email address. Two providers can report the same address without it
being the same person, so the identifier above is what an account is keyed on. The practical effect
is that signing in with a second provider makes a second account until you deliberately join them.

An account also lets a table follow you between devices, which means each of your seats records
which account it belongs to.

## Cookies

Two cookies, both strictly necessary, neither used to track you:

- `corro.session` keeps you signed in, for 30 days from your last visit.
- `corro.external` exists for the few seconds a sign-in is in progress and is then discarded.

The preferences kept in your browser's local storage are settings, not identifiers, and are never
sent anywhere.

## Voice chat

Voice, where a table uses it, is carried live and **is not recorded**. Nothing of what is said is
stored by this deployment.

## Who else is involved

- **The sign-in provider you choose** (Google or Microsoft) learns that you signed in to this site,
  and tells us your identifier, name and address. Their own privacy policy governs their side of it.
- **Microsoft Azure**, where this server and its database run. They hold the data on the
  controller's behalf and do not use it for anything else.

Nothing here is sold, and nothing is used for advertising or profiling.

## How long it is kept

Tables are kept while they are in use and are removed once they fall out of use. An account and
everything in it stays until you erase it.

## What you can ask for

You may ask to see the data held about you, to correct it, to have it deleted, to receive a copy of
it, or to object to it being held at all. Write to **{{contact}}** and you will get an answer within
a month.

You can erase your account yourself, at any time, from **Account settings**: it removes the account,
your address, and every sign-in method linked to it. Signing in again afterwards starts a genuinely
new account rather than reviving the old one.

If you think this deployment is mishandling your data, you can complain to the data-protection
authority for the country you live in.

## Changes

If what this deployment stores changes, this notice changes with it. It describes what actually
happens, and it stops being worth anything the moment it stops matching.
